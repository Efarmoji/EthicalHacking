Chain of Disguises (multi-hop routing)
Attacker → Tor → VPN → Compromised VPS → Bulletproof Hosting → Phishing Page
2. 💣 Ephemeral Infrastructure
1. Hosting phishing pages on hacked websites

2. Auto-deleting servers after a few hours

3. Using burner VPS paid with stolen credit cards or crypto

4. Sending from compromised email accounts, not their own

5. Everything vanishes before anyone investigates.


3. 🧨 Anti-forensics techniques
Sophisticated attackers:

1. Use RAM-only malware (no disk evidence)

2. Pipe logs to /dev/null (don’t store anything)

3. Kill and wipe servers with a single script

4. Use code obfuscation, packing, or steganography to hide payloads

They may even:

Avoid using a keyboard tied to them (e.g., use disposable laptops, VMs, proxies)


4. 🌍 Legal and Geopolitical Barriers
Even if the trail leads to:

... Russia, China, Iran, North Korea, or other countries with poor cybercrime cooperation

It often ends there.

📌 Law enforcement cannot legally force a server host in Russia to hand over logs or arrest someone — especially if the attacker is tied to a state or intelligence unit.
5. 🧪 Operational Security (OpSec) Discipline
The top-tier attackers:

1. Use air-gapped burner laptops

2. Use anonymous crypto (Monero, Wasabi mixing)

3. Create fake identities, forged documents

4. Never log into anything tied to their real name

5. Avoid repeating any unique behavior (mouse, keyboard pattern, etc.)

This is why APT-level actors or experienced cybercriminals can remain free for years — or forever.
